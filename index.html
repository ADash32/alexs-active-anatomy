Fasciae & Compartments

A structural map of superficial fascia, deep fascia, intermuscular septa and a closed limb compartment, tied to the fascia material already used in the reasoning engine.

First-principles fascia reasoning

Superficial fasciaSubcutaneous connective tissue between skin and deeper investing fascia.
Deep fasciaDense investing layer that surrounds muscles and helps organize regions.
Intermuscular septaExtensions of deep fascia that attach toward bone and divide muscle groups into compartments.
Musculovenous pumpConfining fascia limits outward muscle bulging; contraction can compress deep veins and assist venous return.
Compartment syndrome: swelling within a relatively closed deep-fascial compartment raises pressure. The fascia cannot expand freely, so blood flow and/or innervation can become compromised. This is the causal relationship emphasized in the MED 801 fascia material.

Joints & Articulations · Osmosis Reference

Reference + practice based on [Osmosis] Fibrous, cartilage, and synovial joints Transcript. The diagrams are simplified structural schematics: use them to compare the tissue joining the bones, the presence or absence of a synovial cavity, and the motion permitted.
Fibrous jointsSuture; syndesmosis; gomphosis. Generally the least mobile class in this transcript.
Cartilaginous jointsSynchondrosis uses hyaline cartilage; symphysis uses fibrocartilage. Pubic symphysis is the course example.
Synovial jointsFreely movable class with a joint capsule, synovial membrane/fluid and articular hyaline cartilage.
